# Driving in Japan — Okinawa Quick Guide

## Documents to Carry
- Valid Illinois driver's license
- Physical International Driving Permit (IDP) issued under the 1949 Geneva Convention
- Passport
- Rental agreement
- Credit card used for the rental

The IDP does **not** replace your Illinois license. Carry both.

## Drive on the Left
Japan drives on the **left side of the road**. Most Japanese rental cars are right-hand drive, so you will sit on the **right side of the car**.

Helpful mental rule: **keep the driver toward the center line.**

Pay extra attention when:
- Turning at intersections
- Pulling out of parking lots
- Leaving gas stations
- Starting again after a stop
- Entering an unfamiliar road

## Turns
Useful mental comparison:
- **Japanese left turn ≈ American right turn**
- **Japanese right turn ≈ American left turn**

A right turn in Japan crosses opposing traffic, so it is the more complicated turn.

Do **not** turn right on red unless a specific green directional arrow allows it.

## Pedestrians
Pedestrian right-of-way is taken seriously.
- Slow down near marked crosswalks.
- Stop if someone is crossing.
- Stop if someone is clearly preparing to cross.

## Railroad Crossings
Come to a **complete stop** before crossing railroad tracks, even if:
- No train is visible
- The gates are open
- No alarm is sounding

Check both directions before proceeding.

## Speed and Measurements
Japan uses:
- **km/h** for speed
- **kilometers** for distance
- **liters** for fuel

Follow posted speed limits.

## Road Signs
Many major directional signs in Okinawa include English.

Japanese stop signs are usually **red inverted triangles** and may display:

**止まれ**

which means **STOP**.

## Traffic Lights
- No automatic right turn on red
- Directional green arrows allow travel only in the indicated direction
- Be deliberate on right turns across opposing traffic

## Turn Signals and Wipers
Many Japanese cars have:
- **Turn-signal stalk on the right**
- **Wiper stalk on the left**

Do not be surprised if you activate the wipers instead of the signal at first.

## Phones
Do not hold or look at a phone while driving.
- Set navigation before moving
- Have the passenger handle the phone when possible
- A dashboard mount is useful

## Alcohol
Treat driving in Japan as a **zero-alcohol activity**.

## Parking
Do not assume roadside parking is allowed. Use:
- Hotel parking
- Attraction parking
- Designated lots
- Coin parking lots

Some paid lots use a metal locking plate under the car:
1. Enter your parking-space number at the machine.
2. Pay the fee.
3. Wait for the plate to lower.
4. Drive out.

## Okinawa-Specific Tips

### Wet roads
Okinawa pavement can become particularly slick in rain.
- Increase following distance
- Brake earlier
- Accelerate gently
- Slow down before curves

### Naha traffic
Central Naha can be congested. Some roads have restricted **bus lanes** during commuting periods.

### Northern Okinawa
Northern Okinawa is quieter, but roads can be:
- Dark at night
- Curvy
- Narrower
- More rural

Favor daylight for longer drives on your first trip.

## Expressways and ETC
Japan uses an electronic toll system called **ETC**.

When renting:
- Ask for an ETC card
- Use ETC-designated lanes
- Confirm how tolls are settled at return

Do not enter an ETC-only lane without a working ETC card.

## Rental Car Size
For two people, choose a **small automatic compact car**.

Benefits:
- Easier positioning on unfamiliar roads
- Easier parking
- Better fuel economy
- Less stressful on narrow streets

## Insurance
Basic rental insurance may still leave:
- Damage deductibles
- Non Operation Charges (NOC)
- Tire/wheel costs
- Other exclusions

For a first-time driver in Japan, strongly consider the rental company's **highest reasonable protection package**, especially one that covers both the deductible and NOC.

## If You Have an Accident
Even for a minor accident:
1. Stop safely.
2. Check for injuries.
3. Call **110** for police.
4. Call **119** for fire/ambulance if needed.
5. Contact the rental company.
6. Follow the rental company's instructions.

Do not simply exchange information and leave.

## Before Leaving the Rental Lot
- Adjust mirrors and seat
- Confirm navigation
- Find turn signals and wipers
- Check parking brake
- Confirm fuel type
- Check existing scratches/damage
- Locate hazard lights
- Confirm ETC card is installed
- Practice moving in the lot if possible
- Remind yourself: **KEEP LEFT**
